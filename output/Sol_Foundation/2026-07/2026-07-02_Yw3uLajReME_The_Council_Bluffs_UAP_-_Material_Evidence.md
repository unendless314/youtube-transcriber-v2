# The Council Bluffs UAP - Material Evidence

[00:00] On the evening of December 17, 1977, five separate groups of witnesses in Council Bluffs, Iowa, reported the same thing: a red luminous mass descending from the sky close to Big Lake Park.
[00:13] One witness described a round object hovering below the treetops, with red lights around its perimeter. Others described what they found on the ground: a pool of molten metal still running hot at the edges.
[00:28] The city's assistant fire chief arrived within 15 minutes.
[00:31] He found a roughly 4 by 6 foot mass of molten metal still boiling hot.
[00:36] In the weeks following the incident, materials analysis identified the metal as high carbon, steel and slag, products of common industrial processes. The conclusion was essentially: ordinary material, therefore an ordinary explanation. That should have been the end of it, except nobody could explain how a large mass of molten hot steel appeared in a city park, witnessed independently by multiple groups of people with no identifiable source. The case remained open.
[01:07] In 2021, a team of scientists led by Dr. Gary Nolan, professor of pathology at Stanford University, and researcher Jacques Vallée applied advanced materials analysis to samples from the Council Bluffs incident.
[01:21] Secondary iron mass spectroscopy determines a material's exact elemental and isotopic composition at extraordinary sensitivity. The team analyzed the samples at a resolution of 200 by 5 nanometers.
[01:36] These are not 1970s instruments. These are the tools that material scientists use today to work at the edge of what is knowable. The team evaluated five standard explanations: meteorite, satellite debris, aircraft parts, dumped metal, thermite hoax. Every one was ruled out by the physical evidence.
[01:59] No anomalous isotopic values were detected. The material could have been earth sourced. The analysis does not claim something extraordinary happened. But the metals in the once molten mass were incompletely mixed, pointing to an industrial process of unknown origin. No conventional explanation accounts for how it got there.
[02:21] What happened in Council Bluffs is still not fully explained. This paper does not change that.
[02:26] What it demonstrates is that rigorous scientific methods can be applied to UAP incidents, can rule out conventional explanations, and can produce results that hold up to scrutiny.
[02:37] That is what the Sol Foundation exists to do. The full paper is available at thesolfoundation.org.

# The Art of Stopping Emotional Overthinking and Pain

[00:00] When something goes wrong in your life, you are struck by a painful event.
[00:04] Someone speaks to you with disrespect, a relationship ends, or a project you worked hard on falls apart. Naturally, it hurts. But notice what your mind immediately does next.
[00:17] In early Buddhist teachings, the Buddha asked his followers a simple question: "If a person is struck by an arrow, is it painful?" The followers answered, "Yes." The Buddha then asked, "If that person is immediately struck by a second arrow, is that even more painful?"
[00:34] The first arrow represents the unavoidable physical or emotional pain of life, the event itself, which is outside of your control. The second arrow is your reaction to that pain. It is the story you construct around the event. "Why does this always happen to me? I am cursed. Things will never get better." Most of the suffering you feel in daily life does not come from the first arrow.
[00:58] It comes from the dozens of second arrows you shoot into your own heart through overthinking, self-blame, and resentment. You cannot always prevent the first arrow from landing.
[01:10] Life will bring unexpected storms, losses, and disappointments. But the second arrow is entirely optional. When a difficult moment strikes, pause. Feel the initial sting without judgment, take a deep breath and drop the bow. Do not wound yourself twice for a pain that only needed to hurt once.

# How to Stop Taking Things Personally (The Zen Art of Omoyari)

[00:00] When someone hurls a harsh word, a sharp critique, or an unprovoked judgment at you, it stays in your mind long after the room has gone quiet.
[00:09] You repeat the sentence over and over.
[00:12] You feel the sting again and again, carrying their words like a heavy sack of stones everywhere you go.
[00:19] In Japanese culture, there is a profound concept known as omoyari, the art of deep, selfless empathy.
[00:27] We often think empathy is just about being kind to others, but true omoyari gives you the wisdom to understand the hidden pain behind how people act toward you.
[00:38] When someone speaks to you with anger, cruelty, or thoughtlessness, omoyari teaches you to look past the surface of the comment.
[00:46] People do not spill venom because of who you are.
[00:49] They spill venom because their own cup is full of poison.
[00:52] Their words are never a reflection of your worth.
[00:55] And they are a mirror of their own internal chaos, unhealed wounds, and unspoken exhaustion.
[01:02] In Zen, we ask a simple question.
[01:05] If someone offers you a gift and you choose not to accept it, to whom does the gift belong?
[01:11] It stays with the person who brought it.
[01:14] When someone gives you harsh words, you do not have to accept them into your heart.
[01:18] Recognize that their words belong to their burden, not your truth.
[01:22] Look at them with the quiet compassion of omoyari.
[01:25] Wish them peace from afar, lay down their baggage, and walk away with clean hands and a calm mind.

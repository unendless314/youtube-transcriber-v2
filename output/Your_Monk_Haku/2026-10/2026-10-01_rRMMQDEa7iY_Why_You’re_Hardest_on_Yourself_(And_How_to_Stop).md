# Why You’re Hardest on Yourself (And How to Stop)

[00:00] What if the hardest person to forgive, care for, and accept is the one looking back at you in the mirror? We spend so much of our lives offering kindness to everyone else. We listen to our friends' problems, forgive our loved ones' mistakes, and show endless patience to strangers.
[00:15] But when it comes to ourselves, we become ruthless judges. We replay every misstep, highlight every insecurity, and withhold love until we achieve some distant, perfect version of who we think we should be. In Buddhist tradition, there is an ancient practice called metta, or loving kindness. Most people assume metta begins by sending peace to the world or loving your neighbors. But monks have practiced a specific sequence for thousands of years. Metta always begins with yourself. In Buddhist philosophy, you cannot give what you do not have.
[00:49] Trying to love others while hating yourself is like trying to pour water from a completely dry well.
[00:54] The habit is simple, but transformative. Every morning before you look at your phone, place a hand over your heart, take one slow breath, and direct four quiet phrases inward.
[01:06] May I be safe? May I be peaceful? May I be healthy? May I live with ease? At first, your mind will resist. An inner voice might whisper that you haven't earned it yet.
[01:17] But loving yourself isn't a reward for perfection, it is the prerequisite for peace. When you practice offering yourself unconditional friendliness every single morning, you stop treating yourself like a project to be fixed and start treating yourself like a human being to be cherished. Stop waiting to become perfect before you offer yourself grace. Start with yourself today.

# You Do Not Owe the World an Apology for Being Human

[00:00] How many times today have you said, I'm sorry, for simply existing?
[00:04] You apologize when someone bumps into you.
[00:07] You apologize before asking a valid question.
[00:10] You apologize for taking up space, for having a boundary, or for having an emotion that makes someone else slightly uncomfortable.
[00:17] In Zen, an apology is meant to be an act of sacred accountability, a way to repair genuine harm.
[00:25] When you use sorry as a reflex, it is no longer an act of respect for others.
[00:30] It becomes a defense mechanism born out of early conditioning.
[00:34] You learned that making yourself smaller was the fastest way to stay safe and avoid conflict.
[00:40] Every time you offer a fake apology, you send a subtle message to your own subconscious.
[00:46] My presence is a burden, my needs are a mistake.
[00:50] You train yourself to feel guilty for taking up air.
[00:53] To break this habit, you do not need to become harsh or defensive.
[00:57] You simply shift from reflexive apology to quiet gratitude and presence.
[01:03] Instead of saying, sorry for bothering you, say, thank you for your time.
[01:08] Instead of saying, sorry for taking so long, say, thank you for your patience.
[01:14] You do not owe the world a constant apology for being human.
[01:18] Stand tall in your own presence.
[01:20] Honor your boundaries without shame.
[01:23] Reserve your sorry for true mistakes and give the rest of your life your peace.

# The Hidden Trap in Your 3 AM Thoughts

[00:00] It's 3 a.m.
[00:00] You're lying awake, and that one question is looping in your chest.
[00:05] Why is this happening to me?
[00:06] What did I do to deserve this?
[00:09] It's not a philosophy question.
[00:10] It's a heavy, suffocating weight.
[00:13] But let that question sit for a second.
[00:15] What if the pain isn't the trap?
[00:18] What if the trap is the word me?
[00:20] Think of it like a movie theater.
[00:22] When a tragic scene plays, the screen doesn't actually get cut, burned, or broken.
[00:27] The storm is vivid, but the screen beneath it remains completely untouched.
[00:32] Most of us spend our lives treating suffering like a physical attack happening to a fixed, permanent self.
[00:38] We assume there is a solid, separate me standing in the middle of the room, getting hit by life.
[00:43] But Zen asks a radical question.
[00:45] Is that separate self actually there, or is it just an assumption?
[00:49] The dilemma doesn't dissolve by fighting the storm or defending why it's happening.
[00:53] It dissolves when you look away from the screen and question the one who is watching.
[00:58] When you look closely at the anger, the grief, or the heartbreak, you realize they are just passing weather patterns.
[01:04] The permanent, unchangeable you you're trying to protect is just a story you're telling yourself in the dark.
[01:10] The next time suffering shows up and wraps its hands around your throat, don't try to fix it.
[01:15] Just notice, who is the one hurting?
[01:17] The question might not disappear, but the one asking it might start to feel very different.

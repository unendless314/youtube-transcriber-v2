# Your Future Self Is Begging You to Watch This

[00:00] Make the Memories While You Can
 This is a poem I created on my ride back to the hotel after visiting the Super Bowl LX experience in San Francisco.
 Before I say the poem, I wanted to say that San Francisco is a beautiful city.
 It has a perfect balance of preserving history, nature, and being the centre of innovation.
 Visiting Battery Spencer, Chrissy Field, seeing the Victorian homes,
 and enjoying both the views and peace that Coit Tower made me think about how I need to make more memories in my life.
 And this is something you can probably relate to.
 Be honest with yourself.
 How would you describe your life?
 Would you say it was just the mundane things of working, going to the gym, and watching Netflix?
 Or would you say it's a life that is filled with adventures?
 How many unique days can you think of back in 2025?
 When I say unique days, I mean days where you can think of one unique thing that happened that day.
 For me in 2026 alone, I'm grateful to have several unique days.
 Seeing the Golden Gate Bridge in Battery Spencer for the first time was a unique day for me.
 Another unique day was stepping foot on Alcatraz Island for the first time.
 Then there were several more like visiting Monterey, Big Sur, Los Angeles, Yosemite, and more.
 Within those adventures, I was fortunate to have the opportunity to drive a Lucid Air, a Rivian truck, and the world-famous Tesla Cybertruck for the first time.
 In my adventures, I was fortunate to meet people who let me drive those vehicles.
 The experiences were memorable.
 And I'm grateful for the generosity and hospitality of those people.
 Here is the poem that I've written.
 I hope this poem will inspire you.
 We spend a lot of our lives planning for later.
 Later, when things slow down.
 Later, when work is less demanding.
 Later, when we have more money.
 More time.
 More certainty.
 But there's a quiet truth most of us don't want to confront.
 Later is never guaranteed.
 Right now, this version of you, alive, relatively healthy, able to move, laugh, travel, love, and show up.
 That version is temporary.
 Not in a dramatic way.
 Just in the ordinary, human way that applies to all of us.
 And that's exactly why making memories now matters so much.
 When people look back on their lives, they don't measure it in emails sent, meetings attended, or hours worked.
 They remember moments.
 They remember a random road trip that wasn't perfectly planned.
 They remember laughter that hurt their stomach.
 They remember conversations that lasted until 2am.
 They remember the feeling of being fully present here.
 Not distracted.
 Not rushing to the next thing.
 Memories are what remain when everything else fades.
 And yet, many of us live as if we'll get to memories later.
 After responsibilities are handled.
 After stress is gone.
 After we've earned it.
 But life doesn't work that way.
 Here's something worth sitting with.
 Your body will not always feel like it does today.
 Your energy will not always be this available.
 Your freedom will not always be this flexible.
 That's not pessimism.
 That's reality.
 There will come a time, whether because of age, health, or circumstance, when doing certain
 things becomes harder or impossible.
 And by then, what you'll want more than anything, isn't more money or more productivity.
 You'll want more memories.
 We often think memories require something big.
 Expensive trips.
 Major milestones.
 Perfect timing.
 But that's not true.
 Some of the most meaningful memories come from simple decisions.
 Saying yes instead of maybe next time.
 Calling someone you miss.
 Taking a walk without your phone.
 Sitting at the table a little longer.
 Being fully present with someone instead of half listening.
 Memories aren't created by perfection.
 They're created by presence.
 Think about it.
 How many moments have slipped by because we were too busy.
 Too busy to visit.
 Too busy to travel.
 Too busy to slow down.
 Too busy to say how we feel.
 We assume the people we love will always be there.
 That our health will hold.
 That opportunities will repeat themselves.
 Sometimes they do.
 Sometimes they don't.
 And the regret that hurts the most isn't about what we did wrong.
 It's about what we never did at all.
 There's a difference between being responsible and being stuck in postponement.
 Yes we should plan.
 Yes we should work hard.
 Yes we should think about the future.
 But not at the expense of the present.
 Because the present is the only place where memories can actually be made.
 Ask yourself this.
 If someone were to tell you that you had less time than you assumed.
 Would you keep living the same way?
 Would you still postpone joy?
 Would you still delay connection?
 Would you still say, I'll do it someday?
 Or would you choose differently?
 The point isn't fear.
 The point is clarity.
 When we're alive and healthy, we often underestimate how rare that combination actually is.
 Health is invisible when you have it.
 Time feels unlimited when you're young.
 Energy feels permanent until it isn't.
 And that's why now is so valuable.
 Now is when you can move freely.
 Now is when you can laugh without pain.
 Now is when you can explore without limits.
 Now is when memories are easiest to make.
 And easiest to take for granted.
 One day your life will slow down whether you choose it or not.
 Your pace will change.
 Your priorities will shift.
 Your world will get smaller.
 And when that happens, memories will become something you revisit often.
 They'll keep you warm.
 They'll remind you who you were.
 They'll reassure you that you lived, not just existed.
 So the question becomes, what kind of memories are you creating today for the future version of you?
 Making memories doesn't mean abandoning responsibility.
 It means weaving meaning into your life instead of waiting for the perfect moment.
 It means taking the trip even if it's inconvenient.
 Showing up even when it's not optimal.
 Saying I love you without a reason.
 Choosing experiences over excess.
 Prioritizing people over productivity.
 It means understanding that life is not a rehearsal.
 And here's something important.
 You don't need to make memories alone.
 Some of the greatest memories are shared built with friends, family, partners, even strangers who cross your path for a brief moment.
 When you invest in shared experiences, you're not just creating memories for yourself.
 You're creating them for others too.
 You're becoming part of their story.
 And that's one of the most meaningful things a human being can do.
 At the end of your life you won't ask.
 Did I answer all my emails?
 Did I optimize my schedule?
 Did I stay busy enough?
 You'll ask.
 Did I love deeply?
 Did I experience life fully?
 Did I show up while I could?
 Memories are evidence that you did.
 So don't wait for the perfect time.
 Don't wait for permission.
 Don't wait for someday.
 Make the memories while you are alive.
 Make them while you are healthy.
 Make them while you can still say yes without hesitation.
 Because one day, the opportunity to create new memories will quietly turn into the privilege of revisiting old ones.
 And when that day comes, you'll be grateful you lived in a way that gave you something beautiful to remember.

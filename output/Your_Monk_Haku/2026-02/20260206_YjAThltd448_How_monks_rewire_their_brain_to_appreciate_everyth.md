# How monks rewire their brain to appreciate everything #buddhism #mindset #meditation

[00:00] Most of us treat happiness like a destination, something we'll finally reach once we get to
 promotion, find the partner, or finish the to-do list. But neuroscience tells a different story.
 Happiness isn't a trophy. It's a muscle. Our brains have a natural negativity bias,
 a prehistoric survival mechanism designed to look for threats rather than joys.
 To find happiness in the modern world, we have to intentionally rewire that circuitry through
 consistent practice. You can start this process right now by practicing active gratitude.
 Instead of just listing things you're thankful for, take a moment to actually feel the warmth
 of that appreciation, which floods your brain with dopamine. You can also reframe your daily
 stressors by changing your internal I have to into I get to, shifting your perspective from
 pressure to privilege. Finally, make an effort to savor the small wins.
 Spending just 20 seconds fully experiencing a positive moment, like a great cup of coffee or
 a kind word helps bridge the gap from a fleeting thought to a permanent neural pathway. Your brain
 is plastic. It is literally shaped by what you choose to focus on. You don't need a brand new life to be
 happy. You just need a new lens. Start rewiring today.

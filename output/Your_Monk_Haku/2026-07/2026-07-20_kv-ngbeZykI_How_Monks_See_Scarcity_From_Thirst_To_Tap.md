# How Monks See Scarcity From Thirst To Tap

[00:00] Let me tell you how monks see scarcity. Imagine thirst, the dry mouth, the longing for water.
[00:06] Most of us panic in that moment, chasing, grasping, fearing lack. But monks are trained to pause.
[00:13] They see thirst not as an enemy, but as a teacher. Scarcity reveals the truth, that desire multiplies suffering when we fight it. A monk drinks differently. When water is scarce, each sip becomes sacred. The cup is no longer ordinary, its abundance distilled into a single drop. Gratitude transforms scarcity into richness. The lesson is simple. Abundance isn't measured by how much you have, but by how deeply you notice what you already hold. From thirst to tap, the shift is awareness. Scarcity teaches appreciation. Awareness turns a drop into a river. And in that shift, monks discover the paradox: when you stop clinging, even the smallest resource feels infinite.

# Sleep Is Not Productivity Lost — It's Your Soul's Forest Regrowing | Buddhist Monk Sermon

[00:00] There is an hour just before the light fails completely when the forest stops pretending to be busy. The birds quiet themselves without agreement. The wind loses interest in the leaves, and the trees, which all day long have reached and stretched and turned themselves toward whatever light was offered, simply stop reaching. You have felt this hour in your own body and called it laziness. You have felt the pull towards stillness at the end of a long day and named it a failure of will. A debt you will have to repay tomorrow with twice the effort. But no forest apologises for dusk. No forest has ever once felt guilty for the sun going down. The going down was never a mistake, it was the other half of growing. You were taught, somewhere along the way, that a day has value only in what it produces. Emails answered. Tasks completed. A body kept in motion until motion itself becomes the proof that you were worth something. When the eyes grow heavy, you experience this not as your body
[00:54] asking for what it needs. But as your body failing you, you have made an enemy of your own dusk.
[01:00] Look at the tree in the dark. Beneath the surface, the roots are doing the only work that has ever mattered, finding the minerals that daylight could never offer. This is not the absence of labour, this is the labour that makes all the visible labour possible. The tree that never lets its roots do their work does not become a stronger tree. It becomes a tired one. It becomes a hollow one. And then, quietly, a dead one still standing. Your mind too has its roots, and they do not work in the hours you are awake to witness them. It is in sleep that the day's scattered light is gathered and sorted that what you learned is pressed down into the soil of who you are becoming. This is not idleness. You have called yourself disciplined for staying lit long after dusk arrived. But discipline that ignores the season is not discipline. It is only a refusal to listen. The way your own thoughts begin to arrive slightly out of focus, as if seen through water. This is not a personal failing. This is your forest saying
[01:55] the roots have gone too long without the dark. The bear does not apologise for its long winter.
[02:00] It knows the winter was not a pause. It was where its strength for the coming year was quietly assembled.
[02:06] Consider the seed. It does not begin its life reaching toward light. The seed's darkness is not a delay before its life begins. The darkness is where its life begins. Let the lamp go out tonight without apology. Let the reaching stop. Let the leaves fold inward. Let whatever has been growing in the daylight rest against the dark and trust it. The forest, your forest, is regrowing itself exactly as it was always designed to, not despite the night, but because of it, one dark and patient hour at a time.

# Why Fighting Your Bad Habits Makes Them Stronger

[00:00] Japanese monks understand something most of us overlook: the harder you fight a bad habit, the stronger it becomes. Resistance, in their practice, isn't about pushing back.
[00:11] It's about meeting the habit with awareness instead of aggression.
[00:14] When a monk feels distraction, craving, or frustration, they don't try to crush it.
[00:20] They observe it. They let it rise, peak, and fade. Because every habit, no matter how stubborn, is just a pattern of mind. And patterns lose power the moment you stop feeding them with fear or force.
[00:35] This is why monks sit in silence for hours, facing discomfort directly. They learn that discipline isn't born from fighting themselves, it's born from understanding themselves. When you stop treating your habits like enemies, you stop giving them control. So the Buddhist path isn't about defeating your weaknesses. It's about seeing them clearly, accepting their presence, and choosing differently with calm intention. In that quiet resistance, real change begins, not through battle, but through awareness.

# Why Monks Don't Fight Desires They Observe Them

[00:00] Desire is one of the most misunderstood parts of the spiritual path.
[00:03] Most people think monks battle their cravings like warriors, pushing them down, suppressing them, forcing discipline.
[00:11] But that's not how the mind works, and it's not how monks train it.
[00:15] Let me tell you how they actually approach desire.
[00:18] Monks treat desire the way sunlight treats ice, not with force but with warmth.
[00:23] When a craving appears, comfort, praise, food, distraction, they don't attack it, they observe it, they watch how it rises, peaks and fades.
[00:33] And in that quiet watching, something powerful happens.
[00:37] The desire loses its grip, not because it was defeated, but because it was understood.
[00:42] The Buddha taught that fighting desire only feeds it.
[00:46] Resistance gives it energy, but gentle awareness dissolves it.
[00:51] When you sit with a craving without acting on it, you learn its true nature.
[00:55] It's temporary, it's impersonal, and it's not you.
[00:59] This is why monks seem calm, not because they have no desires, but because they've stopped wrestling with them.
[01:05] So the lesson is simple.
[01:06] You don't overcome desire by force.
[01:09] You overcome it by seeing it clearly.
[01:11] And when you see it clearly, it melts.

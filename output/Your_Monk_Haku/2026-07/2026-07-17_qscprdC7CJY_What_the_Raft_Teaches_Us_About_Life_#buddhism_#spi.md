# What the Raft Teaches Us About Life #buddhism #spirituality #enlightenment

[00:00] Let me share a story the Buddha told, one that feels simple at first, but hits deeper the longer you sit with it. A traveller needed to cross a dangerous river. No bridge, no boat, just fast water and sharp rocks. So he built a raft from branches and vines. It was rough, but it carried him safely to the other side. When he reached the shore he looked at the raft and thought, this raft saved my life, I should carry it with me. But the Buddha said, why carry what you no longer need? That's the heart of the story. The raft represents the tools, habits and beliefs that once helped us survive. They protected us, guided us, maybe even saved us. But life changes, and what once carried us forward can eventually hold us back. The Buddha's lesson is simple, use the raft to cross but don't strap it to your back. Let go of what has already served its purpose. Growth requires release. Wisdom requires knowing when to set something
[00:56] down. So ask yourself, what raft are you still carrying? And what might open up if you finally let it rest on the shore?

# Why You Should Read Old Books

[00:00] Most of us spend our lives locked in the now, chasing the latest trends and scrolling through the morning's headlines.
[00:05] But there is a quiet, powerful rebellion available to all of us, the act of reading old books.
[00:10] When you open a book written 50, 100, or 1,000 years ago, you aren't just reading.
[00:16] You are time-traveling.
[00:17] You are stepping out of our modern echo chamber and into a mind that didn't share our specific prejudices, fears, or distractions.
[00:24] Old books provide a vital sense of perspective.
[00:28] They remind us that the unprecedented problems we face today, political upheaval, heartbreak, or the search for meaning, are actually timeless human experiences.
[00:38] As C.S. Lewis famously suggested, we need the clean breeze of the centuries to blow through our minds and sweep away the blind spots of our own era.
[00:47] A new book is a gamble, but a classic is a survivor.
[00:50] If a story has lasted three centuries, it is because it contains a fundamental truth about the human soul that hasn't expired.
[00:58] So, don't just read what everyone else is reading today.
[01:01] Reach for a dusty paperback and let an author from the past challenge your assumptions.
[01:05] After all, if you only read what is current, you'll only think what is common.
[01:09] To truly understand where we are going, we must first listen to those who have already walked the path.

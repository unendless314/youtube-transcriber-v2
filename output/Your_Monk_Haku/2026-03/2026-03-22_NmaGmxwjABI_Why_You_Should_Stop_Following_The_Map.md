# Why You Should Stop Following The Map

[00:00] On my third day in Paris, I made a wrong turn and walked for nearly an hour without recognizing anything around me. I had no map. My phone was dying. The streets curved in ways that gave me no sense of direction. By every measure, I was lost, and it was one of the finest hours of the entire journey. I found a courtyard where a man was repairing a bicycle in complete silence, a bookshop so narrow that two people could not browse it at the same time, a small square with a single tree and three old men playing cards beneath it, unbothered by the rest of the world entirely. None of these things were in any guide. None of them were recommended to me. I found them only because I did not know where I was going.
[00:41] In Buddhist teaching, we speak about the danger of the fixed path. When we are too attached to arriving at a predetermined destination, we close ourselves to everything that exists outside that narrow corridor. Getting lost is not a failure of Navigatio. It is a temporary release from the tyranny of the known. This is just one of ten lessons Paris quietly taught me. Lessons that had nothing to do with landmarks and everything to do with the way we move through life. If this resonates with you, the full sermon is waiting for you. Watch it. I think it will give you something worth carrying.

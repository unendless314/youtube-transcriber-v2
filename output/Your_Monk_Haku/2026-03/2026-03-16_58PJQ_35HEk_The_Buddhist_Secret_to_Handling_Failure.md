# The Buddhist Secret to Handling Failure

[00:00] The deal fell through. The project flopped. The person walked away. And immediately, your brain tells you a lie. This happened because of who I am. We have this corrosive habit of personalizing outcomes we couldn't control. We treat failure as a reveal of our worth, rather than a result of timing, circumstance, or someone else's history. In Buddhism, there's a concept called anatta, the non-self. It suggests that the version of you you're so desperate to protect isn't a solid object. You are a flowing, changing process. When you loosen your grip on the idea of a fixed self, failure loses its power to wound you. It becomes what it actually is. Information. Data. A result that tells you something useful about what to adjust, not something devastating about who you are.
[00:47] Depersonalizing failure doesn't mean you stop caring. It means you create enough space between the result and your identity to look at the situation with clear eyes instead of wounded ones.
[00:58] Collect the lesson. Leave the shame. You are not your last result. You are what you do with it.

# How Paris Taught Me the Art of Presence

[00:00] I stood in front of a painting at the Musée d'Orsay and I could not move.
[00:03] Not because of discipline, not because of training, but because something I was not prepared for stopped me completely.
[00:09] And then I looked around, and I noticed that most of the people passing by were not seeing it.
[00:15] They were photographing it.
[00:16] There is a difference.
[00:17] To photograph something is to say, I will see this later.
[00:20] To truly look is to say, I am here now, and this is enough.
[00:24] The Buddhist teachings speak of the veil of habit.
[00:27] We stop seeing the things we encounter regularly because familiarity creates a kind of blindness.
[00:31] The sunrise becomes background.
[00:33] The face of someone we love becomes furniture.
[00:35] We look without seeing because our minds are already moving toward the next thing.
[00:40] Paris broke that veil for me.
[00:41] A doorway carved with flowers you would never notice in a hurry.
[00:45] Light falling through a narrow street at an angle that exists for perhaps ten minutes a day.
[00:49] These things do not announce themselves.
[00:51] They wait for the one who is paying attention.
[00:53] This is just one moment from a full sermon on ten lessons Paris taught me about presence, impermanence, and the art of truly seeing.

# The Secret Monks Know About Being Alone

[00:00] We often look at a monk sitting alone in a remote cell or a quiet cave and see an overwhelming isolation.
[00:06] In our fast-paced, hyper-connected world, we equate being alone with being lonely.
[00:11] But for a monk living in complete solitude, the experience is precisely the opposite.
[00:16] They do not feel lonely because solitude and loneliness are two entirely different states of being.
[00:23] Loneliness is the painful feeling of absence, the sense that someone or something is missing.
[00:29] Solitude, however, is the rich experience of presence.
[00:32] It is the discovery that when you strip away the constant noise, social performance, and external distractions of daily life, you are not left with empty space.
[00:42] You are left with your true self.
[00:45] In the quiet, monks cultivate a deep living connection, whether to the present moment, to the nature around them, or to a higher sense of purpose.
[00:54] They learn that the human mind only feels empty when it relies on external validation to feel full.
[01:00] Through discipline, breath, and silence, they turn inward and discover an internal sanctuary that is always complete.
[01:07] When you learn to make peace with your own mind, you realize that you are never truly alone.
[01:12] The monk in solitude isn't hiding from the world.
[01:15] They have simply found a companion within themselves that no isolation can take away.
[01:20] True freedom begins when we stop running from the silence and start discovering the quiet strength waiting inside it.

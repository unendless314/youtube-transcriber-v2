# How to Stop Punishing Yourself for Mistakes

[00:00] When you make a mistake, a quiet panic takes over.
[00:03] Your chest tightens, your mind begins to replay the moment on a loop, and an inner voice whispers, Look what you ruined. Look at what this says about you.
[00:12] The hardest part of making a mistake isn't usually the error itself.
[00:16] It's the harsh, relentless self-punishment that follows.
[00:19] We confuse what we did with who we are.
[00:21] We take a single misstep and turn it into a permanent verdict on our character, intelligence, or worth.
[00:27] In Japanese art, there is a practice called kintsugi, where broken pottery is repaired using gold lacquer.
[00:34] Instead of hiding the cracks or throwing the vessel away, the artisan highlights the fractures, making the piece stronger and more beautiful, precisely because it was broken.
[00:44] Handling a mistake without destroying yourself requires adopting that exact same mindset.
[00:50] When you mess up, separate the event from your identity.
[00:54] You made a mistake. You are not a mistake.
[00:57] A blunder is an event in time, not a sentence on your spirit.
[01:02] Instead of asking, "Why am I like this?"
[01:05] Shift your question to, "What is this moment trying to teach me?"
[01:10] Take accountability without taking self-contempt.
[01:13] Fix what can be fixed. Apologize where an apology is due, and then treat yourself with the same compassion you would offer a dear friend who fell down.
[01:22] Growth doesn't come from shaming yourself into perfection.
[01:26] It comes from learning from your missteps and continuing forward with gold in your seams.

# How to ACTUALLY "Sit With Your Feelings"

[00:00] We tell people to sit with their feelings all the time, but almost no one explains how to actually do it.
[00:06] When a heavy emotion hits, anxiety, grief, anger, or deep sadness, our automatic response is to run.
[00:13] We grab our phones, turn on a screen, search for a quick fix, or analyze the feeling until our heads hurt.
[00:20] We treat our emotions like an intruder we have to argue with or kick out of the house.
[00:25] Sitting with your feelings isn't about solving them, it is about staying present while they pass through.
[00:31] To actually sit with an emotion, you have to move your focus out of your head and down into your body.
[00:37] Drop the story about why you feel this way, and look at where the feeling actually lives, physically.
[00:43] Is it a tightness in your chest? A heavy knot in your stomach? A burning heat in your throat?
[00:49] Name it, without judging it.
[00:52] Instead of saying, I shouldn't feel anxious, simply say, anxiety is present right now.
[00:58] Then, make room for it.
[01:01] Unclench your jaw, soften your belly, and breathe around the physical sensation.
[01:06] Imagine sitting next to a close friend who is crying.
[01:09] You wouldn't tell them to hurry up and stop.
[01:11] You would just sit quietly beside them so they don't feel alone.
[01:15] Do that exact same thing for yourself.
[01:18] An emotion is simply a wave of energy moving through your nervous system.
[01:23] It only gets stuck when we fight it or suppress it.
[01:26] When you stop running and allow the feeling to be felt, the wave eventually crests, breaks, and washes away on its own.
[01:33] You don't need to fix your emotions.
[01:35] You just need to give them enough safe space to finish what they came to do.

# The "Self-Improvement" Trap You Fell Into

[00:00] You keep trying to "fix" yourself, but have you noticed something strange?
[00:04] Every time you think you're almost there, your mind invents a new flaw to chase.
[00:10] You clean up one habit, and suddenly three more appear.
[00:14] You heal one wound, and another part of you whispers, "Not enough yet."
[00:19] It feels endless, like you're standing in front of a mirror that only shows what's missing.
[00:26] And yet, here is the part most people never realize: the Buddha never asked you to become perfect before you were allowed to live, love, rest, or begin again.
[00:37] Because the idea of "fixing yourself first" is just another disguise for fear.
[00:42] Fear that you are not worthy as you are. Fear that you must earn your own humanity.
[00:49] The Buddha saw that suffering grows when you treat yourself like a broken object instead of a living being. A cracked bowl still holds water.
[00:58] A crooked tree still gives shade. And a human being, even one who is confused, hurting, or imperfect, is still capable of awakening.
[01:08] You don't need to be fixed before you move forward.
[01:11] You only need to stop believing that your cracks disqualify you from the path.
[01:15] Perhaps the real question is: what part of you would finally breathe if you stop trying to repair yourself first.

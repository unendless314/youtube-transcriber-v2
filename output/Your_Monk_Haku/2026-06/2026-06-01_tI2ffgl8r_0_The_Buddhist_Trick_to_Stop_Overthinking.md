# The Buddhist Trick to Stop Overthinking

[00:00] "It is two in the morning. You are not in danger. Nothing is wrong. The room is quiet, and yet your mind is replaying a conversation from three days ago, and this time you are saying the right thing. But it is not three days ago. It is two in the morning, and nothing you replay will change what was said." The Buddha called this Papanika, the mind's compulsion to take one thought and spin it into ten, ten into a story, a story into an identity. "Here is what I want you to try right now. The next time the loop begins, don't fight it. Don't ask why you're thinking this again. Simply name it. Quietly. This is the loop. Not with frustration. Just recognition. The name is not a wall. It is a light. And in that light, the loop loses its power. Not completely. Just enough.

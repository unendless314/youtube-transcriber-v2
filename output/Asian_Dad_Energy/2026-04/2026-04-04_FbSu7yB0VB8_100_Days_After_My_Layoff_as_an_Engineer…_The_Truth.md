# 100 Days After My Layoff as an Engineer… The Truth

[00:00] Hello world, I'm an unemployed ex-big tech software engineer with over 25 years of experience in the tech industry. It's been over 100 days since my layoff and I've gone through a very interesting mental journey. Now these thoughts and feelings that I've experienced are neither good nor bad but they are very different from before the layoff. So today I'm going to share these thoughts for posterity's sake. So when I was first laid off from my big tech job there was this brief period of disorientation. Prior to the layoff I had already been questioning the reason, meaning, and purpose of my tech career for years but I didn't really have any intention to leave my job or to retire early or anything really. So the layoff came to me as a real surprise. I didn't know what to think or feel about it. When it hit me I was kind of in shock. I was numb to this whole thing.
[00:59] One thing that I did do was to apply to a lot of tech jobs. I applied almost reflexively to these tech jobs. I didn't even question why I was doing it but I applied anyway. Now one emotion that I thankfully did not experience at all was fear. I had been saving and investing my money for years prior to this layoff so my bills were getting paid no matter what. As such I didn't have any financial fears or worries going into this situation. Now I know I'm one of the lucky ones. A lot of laid off engineers do not have the same opportunities or span of time to accumulate money and my heart goes out to all those laid off folks who don't have such a financial cushion. So within about a week the disorientation started to wear off. But here's the thing I have all this time on my hands and nothing to do. So I spent the bulk of that time doom scrolling on social media and gaming and in doing so I started thinking these really negative circular thoughts. After working for so many years in tech
[02:07] it's like work became a part of my identity and so when the job goes away it's like a piece of me went away with it. I started asking myself these super dark questions like what's the point of all of this? All of this studying? All of this work? All of these projects? All of these late nights? Time spent away from my family all just to get laid off. Like who am I without my work? What is the purpose of my life without work? Honestly I got a bit depressed by it all. Now interestingly enough I told my therapist at the time about my dark moods and she was the one who suggested I journal my thoughts as a way of self therapy. And that's how this vlog got started. And you know what? My therapist was right. Just the act of talking about my thoughts and feelings out loud helps a lot. But aside from the sadness I also felt this completely unjustified unreasonable sort of shame. It feels like as if the loss of my job reduced my worth somehow as a human being. [05:04] You look over your shoulders and you see that giant plantation receding in the distance. At this point does it matter whether you were a house slave or a field slave? Should you really feel ashamed of the fact that you didn't get to eat the leftovers from the master's table? Ultimately you're now a free man and the master of your own destiny and everyone else still stuck on that plantation? They're still slaves so that's that. But the thing is I still have a bunch of free time on my hands every day and that free time is a double-edged sword. On the one hand that free time is a blessing and a relief right? I have time to sleep well, eat well, exercise and so on. But on the other hand, I started to get bored. Like really bored. It almost seems like the stresses and challenges of my big tech job had a positive contribution towards my mental health. The freedom to decide whether to continue working on something or to just stop it if you're not interested in doing it, that makes that thing way more fun.
[07:11] This change in turn led to a readjustment of my sense and feeling of accomplishment. What do I mean by this?
[07:20] Well, when I was still working with big tech, I had to constantly deal with big challenges and problems to solve.

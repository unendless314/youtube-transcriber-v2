[03:10] And this leads us to a new system that the world's most important payment companies have just started using. In late 2025, Coinbase officially brought back a long forgotten part of the internet's original design by activating the HTTP 402 status code. This is the "payment required" response that was first planned in 1991 but was never actually widely used. The new system is called X402, and the way it works is truly revolutionary. When an agent asks for something from a website or a digital service, the server can reply with a 402 status message that includes payment instructions right in the response. The agent then quickly signs a stablecoin transaction, sends the same request again with a payment confirmation attached and instantly receives what it asks for. No need to create an account, no monthly bills, no human approval needed, and no monthly invoice balancing. It's just programmable money moving through the same basic system that already powers the entire internet.
[04:09] And what makes this a serious real-world change, not just another experimental crypto project, is the most powerful companies in the world that are supporting it. The system is now managed by the X402 Foundation, which is part of the Lunix Foundation, the same neutral group that manages core internet standards. The list of companies using it includes Google, Stripe, Amazon Web Services, Cloudflare, the Solana Foundation, and surprisingly, Visa and Mastercard themselves. The very companies this new system is set to disrupt. The Coinbase developer program now offers a service that handles X402 payments, supporting major networks like Base, Solana, Polygon, and others. It has a free option for a thousand transactions a month, and after that, the fee is one-tenth of a cent per transaction. To compare, the cheapest credit card transaction in the old system costs roughly 300 times more. And that's before you consider costs for chargebacks, network fees, and the money that banks must keep in reserve.

[12:22] Well, you can think of it as Ethereum's attempt to tackle its biggest obstacle, making the network more scalable without sacrificing decentralization and security. And this is a big deal. So much so that many people are calling Glamsterdam Ethereum's biggest upgrade since the merge. Now, like previous hard fork upgrades, Glamsterdam will implement multiple Ethereum improvement proposals or EIPs simultaneously. Specifically, there are 10 EIPs with two being the most exciting. The first is EIP 7732, which introduces N-Shrine Proposer Building Separation or EPBS for short. In a nutshell, EPBS is designed to make Ethereum's block building process more decentralized and transparent. Right now, many validators rely on external services called relays to connect with specialized block builders who optimize transactions and capture meth. EPBS brings this process directly into Ethereum's protocol, removing the need for these middlemen and making block production more resistant to censorship and
[13:22] centralization. This creates a cleaner system where validators and builders each do their own job, with Ethereum itself handling the handoff and payments. The second headliner is EIP 7928, which introduces block-level access lists or BALS for short. A lot of acronyms here, I know. Anyway, BALS will make transaction executions far more efficient. You see, today Ethereum runs transactions one by one because it doesn't know which bits of data each transaction needs ahead of time. BALS solve this by mapping out the accounts and data each block needs in advance, allowing multiple transactions to be processed in parallel. Think of it like moving from a one-lane road to a multi-lane highway. So, more cars can reach their destination at the same time and there is less congestion during busy times.

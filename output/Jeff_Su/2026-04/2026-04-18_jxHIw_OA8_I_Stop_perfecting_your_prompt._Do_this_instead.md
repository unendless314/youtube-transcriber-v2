# Stop perfecting your prompt. Do this instead

[00:00] Common AI terms simply explain part two. First, grounding. Think of AI without grounding like asking a friend to answer from memory. They might be right, they might be not. But hand them the actual document and say answer from this, and now their answer is grounded in something real.
[00:13] The implication here is whenever accuracy matters, make sure the AI has something to reference.
[00:18] Second, RAG, which stands for Retrieval Augmented Generation. Imagine a student writing an essay.
[00:23] Without RAG, the student writes everything from memory. With RAG, the student goes to the library first, pulls the relevant books, then writes the essay based on what they found. The rule of thumb is, if an AI tool cites its sources, it most likely uses RAG. If it doesn't, it's writing from memory.
[00:37] Third, context engineering. Here's a simple side-by-side. Prompt engineering is putting the right components together. Task, context, tone, to write a good prompt. Context engineering is more about giving the AI everything it needs to work with, background, examples, constraints, and less about the wording of the prompt. So instead of spending time perfecting your question, spend it assembling the right context.

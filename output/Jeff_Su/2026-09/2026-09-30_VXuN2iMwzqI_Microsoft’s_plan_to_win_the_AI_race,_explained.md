# Microsoft’s plan to win the AI race, explained

[00:00] Microsoft just launched the new co-pilot, which is basically their answer to ChatGPT Work, Cloud Code, and Gemini Spark.
[00:06] And after spending two days at Microsoft headquarters and chatting with senior product leaders like Charles LaManna, I think I figured out how they plan to win the AI race.
[00:14] Let me explain.
[00:15] So if you've heard of ChatGPT Work, you know it's an application that runs on ChatGPT models like Astra and Sol.
[00:23] And taken together, the app plus the model is what nerdy AI people like me call a harness.
[00:29] Similarly, Anthropic's Cloud Code runs on Cloud models and Google's Gemini Spark runs on Gemini models.
[00:35] And what's important to know is that all three were originally built with individual users in mind.
[00:41] In contrast, the new co-pilot has two major differences.
[00:44] First, it's modular, meaning you can switch between models from different providers or just leave it on auto mode since co-pilot knows which model is good at what.
[00:53] Second, because co-pilot was designed for enterprises, there's a third middle layer in Microsoft's harness called WorkIQ.
[01:01] For example, if you ask co-pilot for the latest status on a project, the answer might be spread across 50-word docs, 17 channels, and dozens of emails from your manager.
[01:10] WorkIQ looks at thousands of signals from your work to find what actually matters and only uses what you're allowed to see.
[01:18] Put simply, the other three were built originally for a single-player experience, and the new co-pilot was designed from the ground up for entire companies or like multiplayer.
